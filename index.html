THE CORE IDEA

A shared interface
through wrist-camera motion.

Video prediction captures how a task unfolds.
3D geometry turns that prediction into motion.

Robot embodiments differ in kinematics, appearance, and action spaces. A wrist camera offers a common reference: because it is rigidly attached to the end effector, its trajectory determines the corresponding end-effector motion through a fixed hand-eye transformation.

WVP pairs a video generator with a pose estimator adapted from a pretrained 3D model. Model stitching connects the generator’s predicted latents directly to the estimator’s intermediate features, recovering camera trajectories and gripper states without first decoding RGB frames.

Predict future wrist views

The video generator conditions on the current observations and task instruction, producing a visual plan in latent space.

Recover geometric motion

A learned adapter maps video latents into the pose estimator. Camera and gripper heads recover the trajectory and gripper commands for each arm.

Execute, observe, replan

Hand-eye calibration and inverse kinematics convert camera motion to joint targets. The robot executes a short action segment, then replans from new observations.

How the estimator learns

We first adapt a pretrained 3D model using wrist-camera videos and trajectories from five public robot datasets. The resulting RGB expert then supervises the stitched estimator on VAE-encoded videos. This distillation transfers the expert’s motion estimates to latent inputs without additional action labels.

REAL-WORLD EVALUATION

One pose estimator.

We evaluate manipulation across tabletop and laundry-room scenes, including articulated fixtures, transparent objects, and deformable materials. The pose estimator trained on public robot data is reused across Franka Panda, PiPER-X, and Galaxea R1 Pro without further fine-tuning.

COMPARISON WITH BASELINES

Turning video predictions
into effective robot actions.

On four Franka Panda tasks, we compare WVP with VERA, Cosmos3-Nano-DROID, and π0.5 using the same robot setup, camera configuration, and evaluation protocol. Each bar reports success over ten trials.

With VERA-DROID

Using the same video generator, WVP achieves higher task success than VERA’s Jacobian inverse dynamics model on all four tasks. It also outperforms π0.5 and performs comparably to Cosmos3-Nano-DROID.

With Cosmos3-Nano-DROID

We retrain the stitched estimator for the underlying Wan2.2 latent representation. Paired with this video backbone, WVP again improves over the model’s original action head.

WHY MODEL STITCHING?

The same visual plan.
A different recovered motion.

The RGB expert and stitched estimator receive the same video prediction in different forms: decoded frames versus predicted latents. In the lettuce task, the RGB expert estimates mostly forward motion with insufficient descent. The stitched estimator recovers the downward reach needed to grasp the lettuce.

CLOSED-LOOP RECOVERY

Observe the outcome.
Adjust the next prediction.

Execution can deviate from the original visual plan. Replanning from fresh observations lets WVP respond to the state the robot actually reaches.

Retry after a displaced grasp

PiPER-X closes its gripper too early and moves the block. From the updated wrist view, it reopens the gripper, approaches again, and completes the grasp.

Realign with a recessed handle

Galaxea misses the washing-machine handle on its initial approach. Subsequent predictions realign the gripper with the recess before opening the door.
