Abstract

A generalist manipulation policy should perform new tasks, operate in unfamiliar scenes, and transfer skills across robot embodiments. We present Wrist Video Policy (WVP), a closed-loop manipulation framework that represents robot motion through wrist-camera trajectories. By estimating the wrist-camera trajectory, we can recover the corresponding end-effector poses needed for execution through a fixed hand-eye transformation. The framework combines a video generator with a pose estimator. Given a language instruction and current observations, the generator predicts how the wrist views will evolve. To recover the camera motion from these predictions, we adapt a pretrained 3D model using wrist-camera videos and trajectories from five public robot datasets. Model stitching connects the two models in the generator’s latent space, allowing the estimator to recover wrist-camera trajectories without first decoding the video into RGB frames. The resulting pose estimator transfers to Franka, PiperX, and Galaxea R1 Pro without further fine-tuning. Across tabletop and laundry-room settings, WVP generalizes to tasks ranging from precise grasping and stacking to handling deformable objects.

Method

WVP estimates wrist-camera trajectories and gripper openings directly from video latents. A fixed hand-eye transformation maps the predicted camera motion to end-effector targets for closed-loop execution.
